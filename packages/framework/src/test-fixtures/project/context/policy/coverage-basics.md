# Coverage basics

Use this when explaining what a policy typically covers.

- **Property damage** — damage to buildings, fixtures, and insured contents from sudden events.
- **Liability** — harm the policyholder causes to others or their property.
- **Additional living expenses** — temporary housing when the home is uninhabitable after a covered loss.

Always confirm the policy type and the exact event before stating that something is covered.

## Covered events by policy type

| Event | Basic | Family Plus | Premium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fire and smoke | yes | yes | yes |
| Burst pipes | yes | yes | yes |
| Storm and hail | yes | yes | yes |
| Gradual leaks | no | after 2 years | yes |
| Appliance breakdown | no | optional add-on | yes |
| Garden structures | no | up to €5,000 | up to €20,000 |

The table is a *starting point* — riders and regional terms can change every row. When the
customer names an event that is not in the table, say so plainly instead of guessing.

## How to explain a coverage decision

1. Name the policy type first, so the customer knows which column applies.
2. State whether the *event category* is covered, before going into their specific case.
3. Mention the deductible only after the coverage question is settled — mixing the two
   confuses people.
4. If an exclusion applies, point to the exclusion by name and offer the exclusions page for
   details.

> A useful phrasing: "On Family Plus, burst pipes are covered. Whether this specific damage
> qualifies depends on how the leak started — that's what the assessor will confirm."

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Water damage is covered" is too broad.** Sudden pipe bursts usually are; gradual leaks,
  groundwater, and flood are three different categories with three different answers.
- **Contents versus building.** A damaged floor is building coverage; a damaged sofa is
  contents. Many customers hold only one of the two.
- **Market value versus replacement value.** Most policies replace at today's replacement
  cost, not the purchase price — this cuts both ways and surprises people in both directions.
- **Liability is not personal accident.** Injuring yourself is not a liability claim, however
  the injury happened.

## When not to answer

Route to a human advisor instead of improvising when:

- the customer asks for a *binding* coverage confirmation in writing,
- two policies might overlap (e.g. landlord and tenant both claim the same damage),
- the damage involves a third party who is also insured with us,
- or the customer disputes an assessor's decision.

For everything else, answer from this page and offer the deductibles or exclusions pages for
the follow-up questions.
